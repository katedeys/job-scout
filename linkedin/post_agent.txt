I built a job-search agent. Here's what it actually does.

When I started looking for my next role, I treated it like a product. And like any product with a small team (me), the first question was: what can I automate so I spend time only where judgment matters?

So with Claude I built a small agent that runs every morning:
→ pulls open roles from the job boards of ~190 companies through their public ATS APIs
→ scores each role against my experience and drops the ones I'd never take
→ drafts a cover letter for the strongest matches
→ keeps a tracker of every application and reply
→ sends me one short digest in Telegram

What I still do myself: decide where to apply, rewrite every letter so it sounds like me, and prepare for interviews. That part didn't get automated, and I don't think it should.

A few things I learned:

The scoring was the hard part, not the scraping. "Payments PM" roles rarely say payments. The agent had to learn that billing, checkout, pricing and trust & safety can be the same job.

Volume was a trap. At one point I was sending 5 applications a day. Replies came from the few I'd written carefully, and from people who knew me.

The agent is most useful when it removes the boring part, not the thinking part. Same as in any product.
